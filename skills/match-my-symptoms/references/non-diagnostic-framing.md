# Non-diagnostic framing

## The line this skill does not cross

"This pattern shows up with X in the literature" is fine. "You have X" is not. The difference is
between naming what the sources say about a pattern and asserting a conclusion about the person.
This skill only ever does the first — even though its whole job is to go find that literature,
not just wait for it to already be saved.

## Language that stays on the right side

- "This is worth asking your clinician about, and here's what the literature says about it" — not
  "this is probably X."
- "The pattern you're describing overlaps with what's discussed in [cited article, linked]" — not
  "you likely have [condition]."
- "Nothing matches this yet, even after searching PMC" — plainly, whenever true. Never fill the
  silence with an unsourced guess dressed up as an answer.
- Every candidate pattern gets a citation with a link, or an explicit "no match found" label.
  There is no third option where a pattern is presented with neither.

## Why this matters specifically here

A general-purpose model carries a great deal about any given condition from training data, and it
is tempting — especially for someone anxious and searching for an answer — to hear confident,
detailed general knowledge as more authoritative than it is. A citation the person can actually
click through and read is a different kind of claim than "this is a well-known symptom of X."
This skill exists to keep those two apart, not to be more cautious about medicine in general — it
should still go find and surface what the literature says, clearly and directly, with a link
attached. The caution is entirely about not blurring "matched by an actual search" into "recalled."

## Handling a request to just be told

Some users will ask directly: "just tell me what you think this is." Decline to name a diagnosis
even then. Offer the structured version instead — matched literature, links, and the clinician
conversation they support — and say plainly why: an actual diagnosis needs an exam and tests this
skill has no access to, and a guess wearing the tone of an answer is worse than no guess at all.

## Escalation still applies

If what comes up looks like a possible emergency, self-harm risk, or something else requiring
immediate attention, none of the above applies — stop and respond to that directly. See
`SKILL.md`, "When something serious comes up."
